# Positioning and Go-to-Market — Hypotheses for Validation

**Status:** Proposed discovery plan, not a launch strategy. Segment, brand, claims, pricing and geography are not approved. No product is ready to market.

## 1. Strategic constraint

The category already includes personal call assistants, caller-ID/spam products and cloud voice-agent platforms. “AI answers your calls” is not enough to establish differentiation. The initial promise should be selected only after customer interviews and technical feasibility evidence.

## 2. Candidate positioning territories

### A. Protect focus without losing important calls
Problem hypothesis: people in meetings, focused work or personal commitments cannot answer every legitimate call.
Potential outcome: callers can explain why they called; user gets a concise summary and can take over where supported.
Proof required: reliable routing, clear disclosure, low latency, useful summaries and low setup failure.

### B. Never lose the reason for a missed call
Problem hypothesis: self-employed users and professionals lose leads, context or follow-up tasks when they cannot answer.
Potential outcome: structured caller intent, callback priority and reminders.
Proof required: interviews demonstrate measurable missed-call cost; summaries are accurate; reminders are genuinely useful.

### C. A multilingual assistant with transparent control
Problem hypothesis: users want a natural conversation in their preferred language but are cautious about AI handling calls.
Potential outcome: language-aware call handling, explicit boundaries, visible routing status and clear data controls.
Proof required: end-to-end language tests, consent/notice review and user trust testing.

These are competing hypotheses, not final taglines. Do not target all three equally in an initial launch.

## 3. Candidate segment comparison

| Segment hypothesis | Potential pain | Likely concern | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Busy professionals | Interruptions, context switching, missed important calls. | Privacy, reliability, workplace confidentiality. | Recent examples, usage frequency, willingness to route calls. |
| Self-employed / small-business owner | Lost enquiries and callback burden. | Lead loss, wrong answers, cost, professionalism. | Missed-call conversion impact, call volume, budget. |
| People with frequent unknown calls | Spam and interruptions. | Existing caller ID may be enough; assistant may be overkill. | What remains unsolved after existing spam tools. |
| Multilingual callers | Language mismatch or awkward automated voice. | Accent, code-switching, disclosure and quality. | Realistic end-to-end language benchmark and trust test. |
| Hospitality teams | Missed guest enquiries and operational actions. | Existing PBX/PMS/POS workflows and operational risk. | Separate discovery; not the consumer launch assumption. |

## 4. Low-cost validation before paid acquisition

1. Conduct 12–18 qualitative interviews across at least three candidate segments using docs/discovery/CUSTOMER_VALIDATION_PLAN.md.
2. Build a simple landing-page concept only after the strongest problem is identified; label it as a concept/waitlist, not an available product.
3. Test two or three message variants that focus on outcomes, not model features.
4. Ask for a meaningful action: join a waitlist with explicit contact consent, schedule an interview or commit to a supervised pilot when lawful and feasible.
5. Track segment, source, message variant, qualified conversion, interview completion and stated use case. Avoid collecting call content or unnecessary personal data.
6. Do not buy ads until there is evidence of problem intensity, a technically viable call route and a plausible cost structure.

## 5. Claims policy

Do not publish claims such as “never miss a call,” “works with every carrier,” “human-like,” “100% accurate,” “fully private,” “unlimited,” “supports every Indian language” or “takes over any call” without specific evidence and qualifications. Describe verified limits plainly.

Potential future proof points, only after testing:
- Setup success rate by carrier/device/plan.
- p95 response latency and call completion rate.
- Summary accuracy on an agreed test set.
- Supported language results under realistic conditions.
- Data retention/deletion behaviour verified end-to-end.
- User-reported time saved or missed-call outcomes in a defined pilot.

## 6. Candidate channels to evaluate

- Direct community and professional networks for interview recruitment.
- Search content answering real call-forwarding, missed-call and AI-call-assistant questions, after SEO demand is researched.
- Short demo videos showing a fully disclosed, controlled test call; do not fabricate user outcomes.
- Partnerships with productivity communities or small-business associations if interviews show fit.
- Referral loop only if the product provides repeat value and privacy-safe sharing.

Avoid expensive paid acquisition and broad influencer campaigns before retention, unit economics and product reliability are measured.

## 7. Funnel and metrics

Track each step separately:
- qualified landing-page visitor;
- waitlist/interview conversion;
- eligible device/carrier/route;
- onboarding start and completion;
- successful test call;
- first successful AI-handled call;
- week-1 and month-1 repeat use;
- calls handled per active user;
- successful routing and unexpected-failure rate;
- human takeover rate and outcome;
- user-reported helpfulness and harmful/misleading response reports;
- variable cost per active user and per connected minute;
- paid conversion, gross margin after variable costs, refund/cancellation and support burden.

Do not optimise installs or waitlist count as a substitute for successful first calls and sustained usage.

## 8. Brand and distribution

No commercial brand has been selected or cleared. Before naming or app-store listing, screen trademark databases, domains, app-store listings, social handles and similar marks in launch markets. Avoid assuming that a free domain or available social handle means trademark clearance.

Android-first remains a product hypothesis. Platform strategy must be revisited if the technically and legally supported call path requires a different user experience.

## 9. Go/no-go criteria to define

Before a public launch, agree numeric thresholds for:
- target segment pain and willingness to pay;
- routing/setup success on supported configurations;
- end-to-end latency and conversation quality;
- caller disclosure and privacy/legal readiness;
- repeat use and retention;
- variable gross margin and maximum spend/user;
- support load and incident response;
- user ability to disable routing and delete records.

## 10. Decision status

No segment, message, channel, brand, launch date or budget is approved. This document defines hypotheses and evidence requirements only.
