# Market Demand and Segmentation — Initial Evidence Review

**Status:** Initial desk research, not a TAM/SAM/SOM estimate. Date: 2026-10-11. No target segment or launch geography is approved.

## 1. Executive summary

There is a credible category signal for AI-assisted call handling in India, but the size of the addressable market and the willingness of a specific segment to pay remain unproven.

In June 2026, TechCrunch reported that Equal AI raised US$30 million and that the company said it had more than one million monthly active users and over 300,000 daily active users. Business Standard reported similar figures. These are company-reported metrics carried by news outlets, not independently audited data. They suggest real consumer adoption and investor interest in the category, but do not establish retention, revenue, profitability or how many users would choose a new entrant.

TRAI publishes monthly telecom subscription reports. Its December 2025 press release reported 1,162.97 million active wireless subscribers at peak VLR, with a note about a change in inclusion of M2M connections. That is infrastructure-scale context, not a count of unique people who need or would pay for an AI call assistant. Do not use total mobile connections as the product's TAM.

## 4. Candidate segments and jobs-to-be-done

| Segment hypothesis | Job to be done | Why it may be painful | Adoption blockers | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Busy professionals | Let calls explain their purpose while the user is occupied. | Interruptions and context switching. | Workplace confidentiality, trust, carrier setup, wrong responses. | Recent incidents, frequency, willingness to forward calls. |
| Self-employed / small-business owners | Capture legitimate enquiries and callback needs when unavailable. | Lost leads and repeated callbacks. | Caller experience, missed opportunity from assistant errors, price. | Call volume, revenue impact, current workaround, willingness to pay. |
| People with frequent unknown calls | Screen and prioritise calls without answering every one. | Spam, delivery, sales and service calls compete for attention. | Caller ID/spam tools may already solve enough; assistant may be excessive. | Residual unmet need after Truecaller/device features. |
| Multilingual callers | Handle routine calls in a comfortable language. | Callers may use Hindi/regional language/code-switching. | Quality, accent, latency, disclosure and caller acceptance. | Realistic language benchmark and interview evidence. |
| Hospitality operators | Capture guest enquiries and later trigger approved workflows. | Missed bookings/enquiries and staff workload. | Existing PBX/PMS/POS systems, business risk, separate buyer/ROI. | Separate business interviews and API feasibility. |

## 5. Demand validation plan

### Interviews
Conduct 12–18 interviews across at least three candidate segments. Ask about recent call events, current workaround, frequency, consequences, calls that must never be delegated, routing willingness, privacy concerns and past spending. Do not lead with a pitch.

### Behavioural evidence
Where feasible, ask participants to estimate the last 7 or 30 days from call history themselves, without sending call logs or numbers to the research team. Record ranges and anonymized findings rather than sensitive details.

### Concept test
After interviews, test two or three value propositions with a clear disclosure of the concept's unbuilt status. Measure qualified waitlist or interview actions, not likes. Do not advertise a functioning product or collect unnecessary personal information.

### Pilot gate
Only after call-route feasibility and legal/privacy review, run a controlled opt-in pilot. Track setup completion, successful handled calls, user takeover, harmful/misleading outcomes, repeat use, cancellation and cost per active user.

## 6. TAM/SAM/SOM approach — do not invent market size

Do not calculate TAM by multiplying India's subscriber count by a proposed monthly price. Use a bottom-up model after segment evidence:

- TAM: number of people/businesses in a defined target segment with the relevant call-handling job × plausible annual spend, with sourced segment counts and explicit exclusions.
- SAM: portion of TAM with supported geography, language, carrier/route, device and payment eligibility.
- SOM: achievable paid users based on channel reach, conversion, retention, support capacity and budget over a defined horizon.

For every input, record source, year, definition, confidence and whether it is people, SIMs, devices or businesses. Build low/base/high scenarios and avoid double-counting multi-SIM users.

## 7. Initial conclusion

The category merits validation; it is not yet evidence that this product should launch or that the consumer segment is the best starting point. Equal AI's reported usage and funding establish a competitor signal, while Truecaller and other products show that the feature space is not empty. The decisive evidence still missing is segment-specific pain, willingness to route calls, willingness to pay, retention potential, technical feasibility and sustainable unit economics.

## 9. Business phone assistant hypothesis after product pivot — 2026-10-15

The primary hypothesis is now a configurable AI business phone assistant. The earlier Equal AI/consumer assistant evidence is a category signal only; it is not evidence that a small business segment will buy this product. The business case needs its own bottom-up validation.

Candidate segments to compare, not select:
- small service businesses with repetitive inbound enquiries;
- small businesses where missed or inconsistently handled calls may lose leads;
- support/order-status/complaint intake teams with bounded repeatable workflows;
- hospitality operators as a separate cohort because integrations and buyer economics may differ.

For each segment, establish the buyer and operator, inbound call volume and distribution, workflow repeatability, current systems/workaround, cost of failure, data sensitivity, caller-verification needs, route/provider constraints, required connector, support burden and plausible budget. A high call count alone does not prove willingness to pay or automation suitability.

Do not calculate a B2B TAM from total mobile subscribers or consumer assistant metrics. Use a sourced count of businesses in the chosen segment, explicit size/geography filters, estimated eligible call volumes and supported-route constraints, then low/base/high adoption and price scenarios with confidence labels. Keep assumptions separate from sourced facts.
